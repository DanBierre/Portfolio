## Challenge

I wanted something between Notepad and Sublime: quick enough to open a log file, good enough to write code in. Most editors I tried were either too bare or kept interrupting me with sign-ins, licence pop-ups and AI suggestions.

## What it does

Recto has tabs, syntax highlighting, find in files, multiple cursors and a command palette. It also has a Markdown preview and exports to PDF, Word or a web page.

It opens more than text too. Pictures and PDFs open to look at, CSV files show as a table, and Word files show their words.

There are eight themes and three layouts. Page layout puts your writing on a centred sheet, and Focus hides everything else.

## Why it's quiet

Most of Recto's features came from people's gripes about the editors they already use. Recto just doesn't do the annoying bit.

Saving or exporting shows a small note in the corner that fades away, so there's nothing to click. Settings live in the command palette, so you type what you want to change. Nothing gets sent anywhere: your words stay on your computer.

*Recto* is the right-hand page of an open book. It's the page you start writing on.
